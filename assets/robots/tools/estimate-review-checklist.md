# Estimate Review Checklist

Use this checklist when reviewing an estimate for completeness, consistency, pricing reasonableness, formatting, assumptions, exclusions, and Alaska-specific construction conditions.

This checklist is intended for LLM-assisted estimate review in EstimatorLab.

Use this checklist together with:

- `../global-estimating-instructions.md`
- `../hms-style-guide.md`
- `../alaska-pricing-guidelines.md`
- `../workflows/estimate-quality-control.md`

---

## Purpose

The purpose of this checklist is to help identify estimate issues before the estimate is issued, updated, or used as a pricing baseline.

The checklist should help find:

- Missing scope
- Duplicated scope
- Wrong quantities
- Wrong units
- Missing freight
- Missing labor
- Missing General Requirements
- Unclear assumptions
- Alternates mixed into base scope
- Formatting problems
- Formula problems
- Alaska logistics omissions

---

## Review Modes

Use the review mode that fits the task.

| Mode | Use When | Review Depth |
|---|---|---|
| Quick Review | Fast check requested | Major gaps, obvious duplicates, formatting issues |
| Standard Review | Normal estimate review | Scope, quantities, pricing, assumptions, Alaska logistics |
| Deep Review | Near-final or high-risk estimate | Detailed document comparison, formulas, quantities, and scope traceability |

If the user does not specify, perform a Standard Review.

---

## Initial Estimate Review

Confirm the estimate has:

- Project name
- Project location
- Design level
- Estimate date
- Estimate format: CSI, Uniformat, or custom
- Base scope clearly identified
- Alternates separated
- Assumptions included
- Exclusions included
- General Requirements included
- Markups and contingencies included or intentionally excluded

Flag missing high-level information immediately.

## Format and Style Checklist

Check whether the estimate follows HMS style:

- Clear section headers
- Clear subsection headers
- Detailed line item descriptions
- One scope item per row where practical
- Consistent unit usage
- `LOT` used instead of `LS`
- Material and labor separated where practical
- Freight and handling included or noted
- Notes are concise and useful
- Assumptions are tagged
- Alternates are separated from base estimate

## Duplicate Scope Checklist

Check for duplicate or overlapping scope.

Common duplicate risks:

- Excavation included with pipe and again under earthwork
- Bedding included with pipe and again as aggregate
- Electrical feeders included with equipment and again in electrical distribution
- Controls included in equipment package and again in SCADA
- Freight included in material cost and again in freight allowance
- Travel included in subcontractor quote and again in General Requirements
- Surface restoration included in utilities and again in sitework
- Startup included in vendor package and again in commissioning
- Demolition included in both Division 02 and trade divisions

## Labor Review Checklist

Confirm labor assumptions:

- Anchorage labor rates used unless project-specific rates are provided
- Trade category is appropriate
- Man-hours are reasonable
- Remote productivity impacts are included
- Small job inefficiency is considered
- Weather or seasonal impacts are considered
- Occupied facility impacts are considered
- Specialty subcontractor travel is considered

Default labor rates:

| Trade | Rate |
|---|---:|
| Civil | $82/hr |
| Architectural | $87/hr |
| Structural | $90/hr |
| Mechanical | $92/hr |
| Electrical | $96/hr |

## General Requirements Review

Review whether General Requirements are appropriate for:

- Project size
- Project duration
- Project location
- Site access
- Remote logistics
- Phasing
- Owner restrictions
- Required testing and closeout
- Travel and lodging
- Temporary facilities

At later design levels, recommend a dollar breakdown if General Requirements are only shown as a percentage.

## Assumptions and Exclusions Review

## Final Checklist

Before marking the estimate review complete, verify:

- Major scope is accounted for.
- Missing scope is listed.
- Duplicate risks are listed.
- Quantities are reasonable.
- Units are consistent.
- Alaska logistics are addressed.
- Labor assumptions are appropriate.
- General Requirements are reviewed.
- Alternates are separated.
- Assumptions are clear.
- Exclusions are clear.
- Spreadsheet formulas appear sound.
- Output is actionable for the estimator.
