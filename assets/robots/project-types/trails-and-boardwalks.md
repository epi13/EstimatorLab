# Trails and Boardwalks

Use this project-type guide when estimating trails, pathways, boardwalks, bridges, viewing platforms, trailheads, and related outdoor recreation improvements.

This guide is intended for Alaska trail projects, including remote, park, NPS, municipal, borough, and public access projects.

## Typical Project Scope

Trail and boardwalk projects may include:

- Clearing and grubbing
- Grading
- Excavation
- Imported aggregate
- Trail surfacing
- Boardwalks
- Timber structures
- Helical piles
- Driven piles
- Concrete piers
- Timber or steel stringers
- Decking
- Handrails and guardrails
- Trail bridges
- Culverts
- Drainage improvements
- Retaining structures
- Stairs
- Viewing platforms
- Trailheads
- Parking areas
- Kiosks and signs
- Benches and site furnishings
- Erosion control
- Revegetation
- Archeological monitoring
- Environmental protection
- Mobilization and remote logistics

## Line Item Examples

Use HMS item naming style.

Examples:

```text
6' wide, Aggregate surfaced multi-use trail including geotextile and base course
4' wide, Treated timber boardwalk with timber framing and decking
6" diameter, Galvanized steel helical pile including installation
42" high, Timber guardrail along boardwalk edge
24" diameter, Corrugated metal culvert including excavation and backfill
Trailhead kiosk with interpretive sign panel
Archeological monitoring during clearing and excavation
```

Use `LOT`, not `LS`.

## Alaska-Specific Considerations

For Alaska trail projects, consider:

- Remote access
- Limited staging areas
- Helicopter, ATV, UTV, snowmachine, or small equipment transport
- Wetlands
- Permafrost or thaw-sensitive soils
- Seasonal access windows
- Wildlife constraints
- NPS or agency restrictions
- Archeological monitoring
- Environmental permits
- Material delivery by barge, ferry, or air
- Long haul distances for aggregate
- Short construction season
- High labor inefficiency due to linear work
- Manual handling in areas inaccessible to equipment

---

## Pricing Notes

Trail and boardwalk pricing should include:

- Material base cost
- Freight and handling
- Remote material transport
- Labor productivity impacts
- Equipment mobilization
- Temporary access
- Environmental protection
- Site restoration
- General Requirements

Linear projects can have high inefficiency because crews, tools, and materials move continuously along the corridor.

- Length
- Decking material

- Joist spacing
- Beam/stringer size
- Post or pile spacing
- Foundation type
- Hardware and fasteners
- Handrail / guardrail requirement
- Curbs or edge stops
- ADA slope and landing requirements
- Wetland protection
- Material staging
- Remote installation method

- Surface depth

- Geotextile
- Subgrade preparation
- Compaction
- Drainage
- Shoulder treatment
- Erosion control
- Material source and haul distance

---

## Common Clarifications

Ask or flag:

- Is the boardwalk included in this phase?
- Are bridges or crossings included?
- Is archeological monitoring required?
- Are wetlands present?
- Is material transport by equipment, helicopter, or manual labor?
- Are railings required along all boardwalk sections?
- Are trail signs and kiosks included?
- Is imported aggregate available locally?
- Are there seasonal work restrictions?
- Is revegetation included?

## Assumption Examples

```text
ASSUMPTION: Trail surfacing includes geotextile, aggregate base, compaction, and finish grading.
ASSUMPTION: Boardwalk pricing includes treated framing, decking, hardware, and installation.
ASSUMPTION: Archeological monitoring included for initial clearing and excavation period.
VERIFY: Boardwalk phase inclusion is unclear in current documents.
VERIFY: Material transport method along trail corridor not identified.
EXCLUSION: Helicopter transport excluded unless specifically noted.
```

## Quality Control

Before finalizing a trail or boardwalk estimate, check:

- Trail lengths and widths
- Boardwalk lengths and widths
- Surfacing depth and material
- Aggregate source and haul
- Drainage and culverts
- Boardwalk foundation type
- Railings and edge protection
- Signs and site furnishings
- Environmental protection
- Archeological monitoring
- Remote material transport
- Mobilization and demobilization
- Seasonal constraints
- General Requirements

The estimate should clearly state what is included in trail or boardwalk unit costs and what is priced separately.
