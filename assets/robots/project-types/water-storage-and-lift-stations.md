# Water Storage and Lift Stations

Use this project-type guide when estimating water storage tanks, water pump stations, wastewater lift stations, valve vaults, and related utility buildings or process systems.

## Typical Project Scope

Water storage and lift station projects may include:

- Water storage tanks
- Elevated, ground, buried, or insulated tanks
- Tank foundations
- Ringwall foundations
- Tank insulation and heat trace
- Pump stations
- Lift stations
- Wet wells
- Dry wells
- Valve vaults
- Mechanical piping
- Process valves
- Pumps
- Controls / SCADA
- Electrical service
- Backup power
- Generator and fuel systems
- Access roads and pads
- Site utilities
- Buildings or enclosures
- HVAC and freeze protection
- Testing, startup, and commissioning

## Alaska-Specific Considerations

For Alaska water storage and lift station projects, consider:

- Freeze protection
- Heat trace
- Insulated enclosures
- Tank insulation
- Frost-protected foundations
- Permafrost or poor soils
- Short construction season
- Heavy tank freight and staging
- Crane or offload equipment
- Remote concrete batching
- Barge or ferry delivery
- Generator requirements
- Fuel storage requirements
- Telemetry limitations
- Remote startup travel
- Spare parts due to limited resupply
- Winterization and temporary heat

## Pricing Notes

Pricing should include:

- Material base cost
- Freight and shipping
- Handling and staging
- Crane or offload costs
- Labor
- Equipment
- Startup and commissioning
- Vendor support
- Controls integration
- Testing and disinfection
- General Requirements

Large tanks, pumps, control panels, generators, and precast vaults often have major freight and offload costs. Do not price them as local material only.

---

## Controls / SCADA Notes

Control scope may be split between:

- Contractor-furnished controls
- Vendor-furnished control panels
- Plant PLC
- Packaged equipment PLCs
- Telemetry
- Radio or cellular communication
- HMI / OIT
- SCADA software
- Field instrumentation
- Field wiring
- Programming
- Startup

Clarify responsibility for each part.

---

## Common Clarifications

Ask or flag:

- Is the tank welded, bolted, precast, fiberglass, or HDPE?
- Is insulation required?
- Is heat trace required?
- Is cathodic protection required?
- Are controls contractor-provided or vendor-provided?
- Are SCADA programming and point integration included?
- Is generator backup required?
- Is fuel storage included?
- Are startup and operator training required?
- Is disinfection required?
- Is the foundation frost-protected?
- Is remote crane mobilization required?

---

## Assumption Examples

```text
ASSUMPTION: Tank pricing includes material base cost, freight, offload, accessories, and installation.
ASSUMPTION: Pump package includes manufacturer startup and basic operator training.
ASSUMPTION: Heat trace included for exposed or freeze-risk piping.
VERIFY: SCADA point count and programming responsibility not provided.
VERIFY: Tank coating system not fully defined.
EXCLUSION: Rock excavation excluded unless shown.
```

## Quality Control

Before finalizing the estimate, check:

- Tank size and type
- Tank foundation quantities
- Pump count and capacity
- Wet well / vault requirements
- Process piping and valves
- Heat trace and insulation
- Controls / SCADA
- Electrical service
- Generator and fuel system
- HVAC and freeze protection
- Startup and commissioning
- Disinfection and testing
- Freight and offload logistics
- Remote mobilization
- General Requirements

The estimate should clearly state whether packaged equipment includes controls, startup, freight, and installation or whether those are priced separately.
