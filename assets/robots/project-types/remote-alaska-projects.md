# Remote Alaska Projects

Use this project-type guide when estimating any construction project in a remote or logistically difficult Alaska location.

This guide applies across project types, including schools, utilities, water and wastewater systems, trails, public facilities, housing, sitework, and specialty construction.

## Purpose

Remote Alaska projects require different estimating assumptions than road-system projects.

The purpose of this guide is to help the estimator and LLM identify costs related to:

- Freight
- Crew travel
- Lodging or camp
- Per diem
- Equipment mobilization
- Material staging
- Limited suppliers
- Seasonal access
- Weather risk
- Temporary facilities
- Remote productivity loss
- Waste backhaul
- Startup and commissioning travel

Remote logistics should not be hidden inside unexplained unit pricing.

---

## Remote Project Categories

Classify the project location before pricing.

| Category | Description | Typical Pricing Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Road-system regional | Connected by road but outside Anchorage | Moderate freight and labor inefficiency |
| Ferry-served | Requires ferry scheduling | Freight timing and staging risk |
| Barge-served | Coastal or river delivery | High freight, seasonal delivery windows |
| Fly-in | No practical road or barge access | Very high freight and mobilization costs |
| Winter access | Ice road, snow trail, or winter-only route | Seasonal timing and weather risk |
| Wilderness / park access | Limited equipment and environmental restrictions | High labor inefficiency and special logistics |

## General Requirements for Remote Work

Remote projects often require detailed General Requirements.

Check for:

- Project management
- Superintendent
- Safety
- Quality control
- Travel time
- Airfare
- Vehicle rental or local transport
- Lodging
- Camp setup
- Per diem
- Temporary toilets
- Temporary water
- Temporary power
- Temporary heat
- Communications
- Tool mobilization
- Equipment mobilization
- Fuel delivery
- Material staging
- Freight coordination
- Weather protection
- Waste disposal
- Backhaul
- Demobilization

Do not rely on a small percentage for General Requirements if the project is remote and logistics-heavy.

## Freight and Material Handling

Remote freight may include several legs:

1. Supplier to consolidation point
2. Consolidation point to Anchorage or Seattle
3. Anchorage or Seattle to barge/ferry/air terminal
4. Barge/ferry/air transport
5. Local receiving
6. Local trucking or equipment transport
7. Site offload
8. On-site staging
9. Double handling to work area

Each leg can affect cost.

---

## Freight Checklist

Check whether the estimate includes:

- Freight to Anchorage or Seattle
- Barge or ferry freight
- Air freight for small urgent items
- Crating or palletizing
- Consolidation
- Port handling
- Fuel surcharges
- Oversized load charges
- Local delivery
- Forklift, loader, or crane offload
- Weather protection
- Storage
- Double handling
- Missing material contingency

## Labor Productivity

Remote work often reduces productivity.

Labor impacts may come from:

- Travel days
- Weather delays
- Limited daylight
- Limited equipment
- Limited crew size
- Material staging constraints
- Manual material handling
- Short work windows
- Fatigue from remote schedule
- Waiting for materials
- Waiting for inspection or startup support
- Limited local labor availability

Adjust man-hours, not only rates.

---

## Suggested Productivity Multipliers

Use project-specific information when available. When not available, use budgetary judgment.

| Condition | Suggested Multiplier |
|---|---:|
| Road-system regional | 1.05x – 1.15x |
| Southeast ferry / barge dependent | 1.15x – 1.35x |
| Rural barge-served community | 1.25x – 1.60x |
| Fly-in community | 1.40x – 1.90x |
| Winter / shoulder-season work | Add 1.10x – 1.30x |
| Restricted access / park work | Add 1.10x – 1.40x |

Do not stack multipliers blindly. Explain the assumption.

## Equipment Mobilization

Remote equipment costs may include:

- Rental rate
- Delivery to port or air terminal
- Barge or ferry freight
- Local offload
- Local transport
- Fuel
- Operator travel
- Standby time
- Weather downtime
- Demobilization

For short-duration remote work, equipment mobilization may exceed the rental cost.

## Temporary Facilities

Remote projects may require temporary:

- Office trailer
- Storage container
- Heated storage
- Generator
- Fuel tank
- Toilets
- Water supply
- Communications
- Worker lodging or camp
- Waste collection
- Weather enclosures
- Bear fencing or site protection where appropriate

Include these in General Requirements or as project-specific line items.

## Seasonal and Access Constraints

Check whether the project is affected by:

- Barge season
- Ferry schedules
- Winter-only access
- Spring breakup
- Freeze-up
- Wet season
- Snow removal
- Limited daylight
- Fish windows
- Wildlife restrictions
- Park access restrictions
- School calendar constraints

Schedule constraints often affect both direct work and General Requirements.

## Line Item Examples

Use HMS style.

Examples:

```text
Remote project mobilization and demobilization including equipment freight
Crew travel and per diem for remote construction duration
Material freight and handling allowance to project site
Temporary power and heat for remote construction operations
Remote equipment mobilization including barge freight and offload
Vendor startup travel and commissioning allowance
```

Use `LOT`, not `LS`.

---

## Assumption Examples

```text
ASSUMPTION: Pricing includes barge freight, local handling, and on-site staging for major materials.
ASSUMPTION: Labor productivity increased 35% for remote logistics, limited staging, and crew inefficiency.
ASSUMPTION: General Requirements include crew travel, lodging, per diem, temporary facilities, and demobilization.
VERIFY: Final freight route and delivery point not provided.
VERIFY: Local lodging availability not confirmed.
EXCLUSION: Premium air freight for late materials excluded unless noted.
```

## Remote Estimate QC

Before finalizing a remote Alaska estimate, verify:

- Location classification is clear.
- Freight mode is assumed or known.
- Material handling is included.
- Crew travel is included.
- Lodging or camp is included.
- Per diem is included.
- Equipment mobilization is included.
- Temporary facilities are included.
- Fuel and temporary power are included.
- Waste disposal and backhaul are considered.
- Startup and commissioning travel is included.
- Weather and seasonal impacts are addressed.
- Remote labor productivity is reflected.
- Assumptions are clearly stated.

Remote project estimates should explain the logistics basis enough that another estimator can understand how the pricing was built.
