# Historic Restoration

Use this project-type guide when estimating historic restoration, preservation, rehabilitation, repair, and adaptive reuse work.

This guide is intended for Alaska projects involving historic buildings, cultural resources, public facilities, parks, federal properties, remote heritage structures, and existing facilities where original fabric must be protected.

- Existing condition documentation

- Salvage and reinstallation
- Protection of historic fabric
- Temporary shoring
- Structural stabilization
- Foundation repair
- Masonry restoration

- Wood siding restoration
- Window restoration
- Door restoration
- Roofing restoration
- Exterior painting
- Interior finish restoration
- Plaster repair
- Millwork repair

## Alaska-Specific Considerations

For Alaska historic restoration projects, consider:

- Remote access
- Limited specialty trades
- Weather exposure
- Short construction season
- Freeze-thaw damage
- Moisture intrusion
- Log or timber deterioration
- Foundation movement from frost or poor soils
- Freight for custom materials
- Long lead times for specialty products
- Need to protect original fabric during transport and storage
- Limited availability of compatible historic materials
- Remote consultant travel
- Remote inspection travel

## Historic Preservation Approach

Historic restoration estimates should distinguish between:

- Remove and replace
- Repair in place
- Salvage and reinstall
- Restore original material
- Replicate missing material
- Stabilize only
- Protect in place
- Document and remove

Do not assume replacement when restoration is required.

Do not assume standard demolition productivity for selective historic removals.

---

## Labor and Productivity Notes

Historic work is usually less productive than standard new construction.

Labor may be affected by:

- Handwork
- Careful removals
- Salvage requirements
- Unknown existing conditions
- Limited access
- Protection requirements
- Specialty craft labor
- Mockup approval
- Preservation review
- Documentation requirements

Increase labor hours where appropriate and explain the assumption.

---

## Hazardous Materials Notes

Historic buildings may contain:

- Lead-based paint
- Asbestos-containing materials
- Mold
- Mercury switches
- PCB-containing materials
- Contaminated soils

If hazmat reports are not provided, flag the issue.

Do not include abatement unless directed, but do not ignore the possibility.

---

## Common Clarifications

Ask or flag:

- Is the intent restoration, rehabilitation, repair, or replacement?
- Which elements must be salvaged?
- Are original materials to be reused?
- Are mockups required?
- Are preservation standards specified?
- Is hazardous materials abatement included?
- Are existing condition surveys complete?
- Are hidden condition allowances required?
- Are specialty craft trades available locally?
- Is work subject to SHPO, NPS, or other preservation review?
- Are custom materials or profiles required?

## Assumption Examples

```text
ASSUMPTION: Pricing assumes selective demolition with protection of adjacent historic fabric.
ASSUMPTION: Window pricing assumes restoration of existing sash, glazing repair, paint preparation, and reinstallation of salvageable hardware.
ASSUMPTION: Includes budgetary allowance for hidden condition repairs typical of historic restoration work.
VERIFY: Hazardous materials report not provided.
VERIFY: Preservation review and mockup requirements not fully defined.
EXCLUSION: Hazardous materials abatement excluded unless specifically identified.
```

## Pricing Notes

Historic restoration pricing should include:

- Protection
- Documentation
- Selective demolition inefficiency
- Salvage handling
- Specialty labor
- Material matching
- Mockups
- Storage
- Freight for specialty materials
- Hidden condition allowance
- Consultant or inspection coordination where required
- General Requirements

Avoid pricing historic work as standard new construction unless documents clearly call for replacement with modern materials.

---

## Quality Control

Before finalizing a historic restoration estimate, check:

- Existing condition documentation
- Protection requirements
- Salvage requirements
- Selective demolition
- Specialty material matching
- Mockups
- Hidden condition allowances
- Hazardous materials assumptions
- Preservation review requirements
- Labor productivity adjustments
- Freight and remote logistics
- Weather protection
- Closeout documentation
- General Requirements

The estimate should clearly state where restoration is assumed, where replacement is assumed, and where verification is needed.
