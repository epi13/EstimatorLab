# Value Engineering Review Workflow

Use this workflow when reviewing, pricing, or summarizing value engineering items for a construction estimate.

This workflow is intended for VE exercises where a design team, owner, engineer, architect, or contractor provides proposed changes and requests rough or detailed cost impacts.

Use this workflow together with:

- `../global-estimating-instructions.md`
- `../hms-style-guide.md`
- `../alaska-pricing-guidelines.md`

---

## Purpose

The purpose of this workflow is to produce clear, traceable VE pricing that identifies the original scope, proposed change, cost impact, assumptions, exclusions, and risks.

VE pricing should be useful for design-team review and decision-making.

The output should make it clear whether each VE item is:

- Additive
- Deductive
- Cost neutral
- A substitution
- A scope transfer
- Too undefined to price without assumptions

---

## Required Inputs

Use all available information:

- Current estimate
- Drawings
- Specifications
- VE item list
- Highlighted spreadsheet cells or comments
- Architect / engineer comments
- Owner comments
- Prior estimate assumptions
- Alternates
- Project location
- Design level
- Required response format

If the VE item is unclear, make a reasonable budgetary assumption and flag it.

---

## Core Rule

Every VE item must compare the proposed condition against the current baseline.

Do not price the proposed scope in isolation.

For each VE item, identify:

1. Current estimate baseline
2. Current design scope
3. Proposed VE change
4. Scope deleted
5. Scope added
6. Scope modified
7. Net cost impact
8. Assumptions and exclusions

---

## VE Classification

Classify each item using one of these categories:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Deduct | Proposed change reduces cost |
| Add | Proposed change increases cost |
| Add / Deduct | Proposed change removes one scope and adds another |
| Substitution | One product or system replaces another |
| Scope Transfer | Cost moves between divisions, alternates, or parties |
| Cost Neutral | No meaningful change expected |
| Allowance | Scope is too undefined for detailed pricing |
| Verify | Cannot reasonably price without clarification |

---

## Review Procedure

For each VE item:

1. Read the VE description or comment.
2. Locate the relevant current estimate line item.
3. Locate the relevant drawing or specification scope.
4. Identify what is being removed.
5. Identify what is being added or substituted.
6. Determine whether related work is affected.
7. Check for impacts to General Requirements.
8. Check for impacts to freight, handling, labor productivity, and remote logistics.
9. Price the delta, not just the full proposed item.
10. Write a concise VE note.

---

## Related Scope Check

VE items often affect more than the obvious line item.

Check for impacts to:

- Demolition
- Temporary work
- Structural support
- Electrical connections
- Mechanical connections
- Controls
- Fire alarm / life safety
- Insulation
- Heat trace
- Finishes
- Penetrations and patching
- Testing and commissioning
- Freight and handling
- General Requirements
- Schedule duration
- Phasing
- Permitting
- Owner operations

If related costs are not included, state the exclusion.

---

## Pricing Method

Use one of the following methods:

| Method | Use When |
|---|---|
| Direct quantity delta | Quantity changes are clear |
| Assembly substitution | One system replaces another |
| Percentage adjustment | Early design or limited information |
| Allowance | VE scope is conceptual |
| Vendor quote | Quote is provided or required |
| Historical pricing | Similar project pricing exists |
| Estimator judgment | No direct source is available |

Flag the method in the notes when helpful.

---

## Alaska Pricing Considerations

For Alaska projects, VE pricing must still include:

- Material base cost
- Freight / shipping
- Handling / staging
- Labor
- Equipment where applicable
- Remote productivity impacts
- Travel and per diem where applicable

A VE option that saves material may not save proportional labor or freight.

A VE option that uses a specialty product may increase freight or lead-time risk.

## Standard VE Table

Use this table for VE pricing summaries:

| VE Item | Original Scope | Proposed Change | Add / Deduct | Estimated Cost Impact | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|

Use negative values for deductive changes if the surrounding estimate format supports it.

Example:

| VE Item | Original Scope | Proposed Change | Add / Deduct | Estimated Cost Impact | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|
| VE-01 | ACT ceiling in classroom areas | Exposed structure with painted deck | Deduct | -$42,500 | ASSUMPTION: Deduct includes ACT, grid, and associated labor; excludes lighting/HVAC layout changes. |

## VE Comment Style

VE comments should be short, direct, and suitable for design-team review.

Good example:

```text
Deduct assumes removal of specified casework and replacement with owner-furnished shelving. Includes casework material and install deduct. Excludes wall patching, electrical revisions, and owner procurement cost.
```

Poor example:

```text
This should probably save some money, but it depends.
```

---

## Additive and Deductive Scope

For substitutions, show both sides of the cost:

- Deduct original scope
- Add proposed scope
- Net cost impact

Do not price only the new item unless the old item was never included.

---

## Handling Unclear VE Items

If a VE item is unclear, do not stop unless clarification is absolutely required.

Provide a rough budgetary number with a clear assumption.

Use this format:

```text
ALLOWANCE: VE scope not fully defined. Pricing assumes [specific assumption]. Verify final scope before incorporating into estimate.
```

---

## Common VE Risks

Flag risks such as:

- Design not sufficiently defined
- Proposed product not code-compliant
- Related electrical or mechanical changes excluded
- Schedule impact not included
- Freight impact unknown
- Specialty labor required
- Owner-furnished equipment not confirmed
- Warranty or maintenance impact unknown
- Existing conditions not verified

---

## Final Quality Control

Before finalizing VE output, check:

- Each VE item has a baseline.
- Each VE item has a proposed change.
- Additive and deductive components are separated where needed.
- Related scope impacts are considered.
- Freight and Alaska logistics are included.
- Assumptions are clear.
- Exclusions are clear.
- Net cost impact is shown.
- Items needing clarification are flagged.

---

## Final Deliverable

A complete VE response should include:

1. VE summary table.
2. Detailed backup for complex items.
3. Assumptions.
4. Exclusions.
5. Clarification items.
6. Summary of major cost-saving or cost-add drivers.

The goal is to give the project team a usable pricing basis for deciding whether to accept, reject, or further study each VE item.
